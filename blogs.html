How to pack for a week by the sea

A carry-on is enough for seven days on the coast if you plan around three colours and one pair of good sandals.

Start with the things you will wear every day: two swimsuits, a linen shirt, light trousers and a cover-up that works at the pool and at dinner. Pick pieces that mix with each other so every top goes with every bottom.

Leave the bulky items at home. The hotel provides beach towels, bathrobes, a hairdryer and reef-safe sunscreen at the pool bar, so there is no need to pack them.

Finally, bring a small foldable bag for the beach and the market. It weighs nothing and saves you from buying one on the first day.

A new summer menu from our kitchen

Our chef rewrote the menu around what local farmers and fishermen bring to the back door each morning.

The new menu is short on purpose: six starters, six mains and three desserts. Everything changes with the season, and the fish of the day depends on the morning catch.

Guests' favourites from last year stay on the list, including the grilled octopus with fava and the olive oil cake with orange syrup. New this summer are a cold tomato soup and slow-cooked lamb with herbs from our garden.

The restaurant is open for dinner from 19:00 to 23:00. Tables by the water fill up quickly, so ask the front desk to reserve one when you check in.

Three evening rituals for better sleep on holiday

New beds and warm nights can make the first nights restless. These small habits help guests settle in faster.

Take a short walk after dinner. Twenty minutes along the shore helps digestion and slows the day down before bed.

Keep screens away for the last half hour. Our rooms have blackout curtains and a pillow menu at the front desk; try the buckwheat pillow if you like a firmer feel.

Book an evening massage at the spa. The last treatment starts at 20:00, and guests who try it often say it is the best night's sleep of their stay.

Our lobby has a new look

After a winter of work, the lobby reopens with more light, more plants and a quiet corner for reading.

We opened up the old arches to let in the morning sun and replaced the heavy furniture with lighter armchairs made by a local workshop.

The new reading corner has a small library of travel books and guides to the region. Borrow any book you like and leave it at the front desk when you go.
